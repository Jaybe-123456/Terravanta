# Cooperative receivables discovery and pilot kit

This kit is for discovering whether a cooperative has a costly, unresolved processor receivable workflow that a current MIS does not already solve. It is a discovery and controlled test plan, not evidence that Terravanta has a customer, a legal claim, or proven faster payment. No willing pilot buyer or validated cooperative/processor relationship is in place yet.

## 20-minute discovery conversation

Ask about one **specific completed or overdue processor-period claim**. Keep the first conversation about the existing work; do not lead with a demo or ask whether they “like” Terravanta.

1. “Walk me through one amount you say a processor still owes, from contract and supply records through acceptance, statement/invoice and cash received.”
2. “Which systems or files contain each step? What source wins when your records and the processor statement differ?”
3. “How do you calculate its payment due date from the actual contract, and who reviews that term?”
4. “What part is accepted by the processor, disputed, unsupported or simply unpaid due to liquidity?”
5. “Who follows up, what evidence do they send, how long does assembly take, and when did the processor respond?”
6. “How are quality adjustments, deductions, credit notes and partial bank/M-Pesa receipts matched to the claim?”
7. “What does your current MIS, processor portal, e-invoice or accountant already do? What is still manual?”
8. “Does this happen for multiple settlement periods? What staff time or external cost does it consume?”
9. “Who owns a budget for this workflow, and what would a priced repeat service need to prove?”
10. “Would you be willing to invite the processor's AP/finance owner to review a source-linked claim response workflow?”

Close with a concrete ask: “Would you authorize a scoped, retrospective reconstruction of one claim and ask the processor to confirm, partially accept or dispute it, then decide whether the measured evidence value merits a paid repeat?” If the owner says no, ask which incumbent system or current process already does the job.

Record answers as attributed observations, not assumptions. Do not record names of members, account details, exact payout amounts or confidential internal controls in ordinary discovery notes.

## Baseline to capture before Terravanta

Agree the specific claim and definitions with the cooperative before timing anything. Record the current process first:

| Measure | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Claim assembly elapsed time | Time from the latest required source becoming available to a complete claim package being ready to send. Keep the date and working-time basis. |
| Active staff time | Minutes by role/source: contract lookup, data export, matching, evidence chase, review and follow-up. |
| Asserted amount and basis | Cooperative's stated amount per invoice/period and every source used; record unsupported components explicitly. |
| Counterparty position | Amount accepted, partly accepted, disputed, rejected or unanswered; preserve processor's stated reason and response date. |
| Due date and age | The actual reviewed term/date and the as-of date. If contract or term is missing/unclear, record “not established.” Do not use milk delivery date as overdue age. |
| Cash receipts | Dates and references for actual receipts, allocated to claims only once; distinguish seller-entered receipt from bank/processor evidence. |
| Rework and follow-up | Handoffs, repeated requests, missing evidence, correction effort and response time. Do not infer why payment is late. |
| Existing spend | Accounting/legal/collection/financing software or service cost if disclosed; do not estimate labor cost without buyer-provided rates. |

During the Terravanta run, record the same measures plus mapping time, source coverage, provenance gaps, false matches, number of amount lines confirmed/disputed by the processor, receipt-allocation checks, export corrections, counterparty follow-up and support cost. No automatic match rate proves claim validity. Have the cooperative validate its records and ask the processor to validate its position.

## Data and test safeguards

1. Use one already completed/overdue period. The retrospective test must not change a live payout, ledger, contract or member record.
2. Obtain written approval from the cooperative's authorized owner for the specific files, users, purpose, storage location, retention period and deletion method. Use their approved transfer channel. Do not send files to this chat, ordinary email, or an external AI model.
3. Minimize member detail; pseudonymize member identifiers if claim evidence can be reconstructed at cooperative/processor aggregate level. Keep the crosswalk with the cooperative.
4. Remove names, phone numbers, national IDs, account/payment identifiers not required to prove the claim, and unrelated columns. Keep only approved fields and preserve enough source locators for audit.
5. Pseudonymized financial values and processor references can still identify commercial relationships; treat them as confidential and re-identifiable.
6. The receivables workflow now has role-checked uploads, private local-disk or S3-compatible storage, PDF/CSV type and size checks, SHA-256 verification on download/export, and case-scoped access. The Render Blueprint's default is a single-instance private persistent disk. Malware scanning, automatic expiry/deletion, tested backup/restore and a formal security review are not yet implemented. **Do not upload a cooperative's files until the updated API/schema is deployed, the storage/backup/retention configuration is reviewed, and written authorization exists.**
7. Cooperative validates seller-side evidence; authorized processor user supplies its position. Terravanta's status is a workflow record, not a legal determination, audit opinion, bank verification or debt certification.

## Paid-pilot decision

Do not quote an invented market price or promise faster payment or recovery. First establish the budget owner, current tool/service spend, claim frequency, evidence preparation cost, counterparty engagement and Terravanta cost-to-serve. Then offer a fixed-scope, **paid** one-claim / one-period pilot with source setup priced separately. Ask for a decision date. Free use, finance introductions or positive feedback are not evidence of willingness to pay.

Proceed to a second, current-cycle paid claim period only if all of these are true:

- A real cross-system claim exists and the existing tool does not already resolve the work.
- Cooperative and processor roles/authority and a budget owner are named; the processor can respond.
- Cooperative authorizes each source and approves a secure, tested transfer/retention/deletion plan.
- Seller assertion, processor-confirmed amount, disputed amount and actual receipts remain distinct.
- The buyer-defined evidence/time threshold is met and the budget owner agrees to pay for the repeat.
- Mapping, human review, processor follow-up and security costs are known.

Stop or change the target if the current stack already proves/ages claims, the processor will not participate and a buyer does not value a seller-side pack, data authority is unclear, or no paid route emerges. Test a second relationship only after the first buyer chooses a paid repeat.

## Current product readiness

The current settlement-close CSV workflow is not a processor-receivable ledger. A manual receivables API/UI now supports claim drafts, seller assertion, processor submission and responses, due-date aging, receipt allocations, a ZIP evidence export and an attested budget-owner decision record. It has no real cooperative/processor claim, actual processor response, validated paid-repeat decision or source adapter. Contract management, malware scanning, retention/deletion automation and tested restore behavior remain incomplete. Keep all claim data synthetic or use no data until deployment, authorization and customer-approved operating controls have been checked.
